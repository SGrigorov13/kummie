# KUMMIE — Final Platform Concept

**Brand:** KUMMIE  
**Tagline:** **Your K-pop Chingu**  
**Concept:** European K-pop commerce + media + fan community + collector ecosystem + future AI companion  
**Starting point:** Bulgaria  
**Launch date:** October 2026  
**Planning horizon:** October 2026 – September 2029 (36 months)  
**Initial physical model:** Online-first; no permanent physical store  
**Fulfilment model:** Hybrid supplier-direct + EU fulfilment as volume develops

> **Important:** This document is a business concept and planning model, not legal or tax advice. Product-specific compliance, trademark clearance, VAT setup, contracts and employment arrangements should be reviewed with qualified Bulgarian/EU professionals before launch.

---

## 1. Executive concept

KUMMIE should not be built as another generic K-pop webshop. The intended business is a **fan-first digital platform** that combines:

1. **E-commerce** — official/licensed K-pop merchandise and carefully selected original K-pop-inspired products.
2. **Media/blog** — useful, searchable K-pop content that attracts organic traffic and builds trust.
3. **Teen editorial** — a parent-controlled, privacy-first section created with input from a young K-pop fan (the founder's daughter), without making the child's identity public.
4. **Collector ecosystem** — photocards, binders, sleeves, displays, storage and collecting guidance.
5. **Community** — wishlists, collections, reviews, events and loyalty features added progressively.
6. **AI companion** — a future KUMMIE assistant that helps fans discover releases, products, gifts, collecting supplies and content.

The strategic objective is to move from a **reseller model** toward a **proprietary customer relationship and platform**.

### Brand promise

> **KUMMIE — Your K-pop Chingu**
>
> *A friendly European place to discover, collect and enjoy K-pop.*

**Chingu (친구)** is the Korean word commonly used for “friend”. The brand name KUMMIE is an invented brand name; the Korean connection is carried by the tagline rather than by claiming that “Kummie” is a Korean word.

# 2. Why KUMMIE can work

## 2.1 Market logic

European K-pop commerce is already competitive. Established specialist retailers demonstrate that there is a functioning market for albums, official merchandise, lightsticks, pre-orders, posters, DVDs, photocards and related products. Nolae, for example, operates a European K-pop shop with EU shipping, loyalty points and European fulfilment; Koreapopstore operates a Korea-based B2B wholesale model for retailers and business buyers. [Sources: Nolae](https://nolae.eu/), [Koreapopstore](https://koreapopstore.com/)

Therefore KUMMIE should not try to win only through:

- the largest catalogue;
- the lowest price;
- or “we sell every group”.

The differentiation should be:

**content + discovery + collection tools + convenience + regional relevance + community + own-brand products.**

## 2.2 The economic opportunity

Official albums can be comparatively price-transparent and therefore may carry lower gross margins than own-brand accessories. Korean B2B examples show meaningful wholesale discounts, but European retail economics must additionally absorb freight, payment costs, fulfilment, returns, VAT and customer acquisition. [Koreapopstore wholesale](https://www.koreapopstore.com/how-to/wholesale.html)

Consequently the portfolio should deliberately mix:

- **lower-margin traffic/credibility products:** official albums;
- **medium-margin hero products:** official MD and lightsticks;
- **higher-margin basket builders:** collector accessories;
- **higher-margin proprietary products:** KUMMIE-brand goods and original designs.

# 6. Phase 2 product expansion — “unofficial” category

The proposed second-phase expansion can materially improve revenue and margin, **but “unofficial” must not be treated as synonymous with “free to copy”.**

### Safe commercial direction

KUMMIE can sell **original K-pop-inspired/fan-oriented products** that do not reproduce third-party protected names, logos, photographs, likenesses, album artwork, character artwork or other protected material without authorization.

Examples from the planned category list:

- generic/original fan cards;
- calendars with original artwork or properly licensed content;
- original posters;
- original collectible figures/charms where no third-party IP is copied;
- phone cases;
- tablet cases;
- laptop cases;
- mouse pads;
- backpacks;
- water bottles;
- pens;
- pencil cases;
- school boxes;
- stickers;
- keyrings;
- desk organizers;
- collector storage;
- original printed apparel;
- caps/hats;
- sweatshirts;
- T-shirts.

### What requires licensing

Products using a specific artist/group's:

- name;
- logo;
- official slogan;
- photograph;
- likeness;
- character;
- album artwork;
- protected graphic;
- trademarked visual identity;

should be treated as **licensed IP merchandise** unless qualified counsel confirms a specific legal basis for the use.

The intended business policy is therefore:

> **KUMMIE Originals = original designs.**
>
> **Official = licensed/authentic products.**
>
> **Fan/IP products = only after rights clearance/licensing.**

This keeps the high-margin product strategy while avoiding making infringement a core business model.

| Official albums | 25–40% | Traffic/repeat demand |
| Official lightsticks | 35–50% | High-value fandom product |

| KUMMIE Originals | 55–70% | Proprietary margin |
| Original apparel | 55–70% | Brand building + margin |
| Bundles | 50–65% | AOV improvement |

These are KUMMIE planning targets, not published industry averages.

### Core pricing formula

Use **landed cost**, not supplier list price, as the starting point.

`Landed cost = supplier product cost + inbound freight + customs/duty + import/handling fees + relevant packaging/processing costs`

Then:

`Gross margin = (selling price excluding VAT - landed cost) / selling price excluding VAT`

`Markup = (selling price excluding VAT - landed cost) / landed cost`

Do not confuse markup with gross margin.

# 8. Automated order flow

## Desired launch workflow

### Step 1 — Customer buys on KUMMIE

Customer selects products and completes checkout.

### Step 2 — Payment

Payment is processed through the selected payment stack, e.g. Shopify Payments with supported wallets such as Apple Pay and Google Pay where available.

### Step 3 — Automatic supplier order

The order is automatically transmitted to the K-pop supplier/fulfilment partner through an API, app/integration, EDI, email automation or other reliable workflow.

### Step 4 — Supplier fulfilment

Supplier packs and ships directly to the customer in the initial target corridor:

- Bulgaria
- Romania
- Greece
- Croatia
- Slovenia
- Slovakia
- Hungary
- Poland
- Czechia

### Step 5 — Tracking

Supplier/carrier tracking is automatically returned to the KUMMIE system and emailed/pushed to the customer.

## Critical operating condition

The direct-from-Asia model should only be used where the supplier can support KUMMIE's EU compliance, customs and customer-service requirements.

From **1 July 2026**, the EU introduced a temporary €3 customs duty for low-value parcels imported from outside the EU in the relevant e-commerce arrangements, making unmanaged direct-to-consumer Asian shipping less attractive. The duty is applied per item/tariff classification under the measure. [European Commission — €3 customs duty for low-value parcels](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/ensuring-fairness-and-safety-eur3-customs-duty-low-value-parcels-2026-06-29_en)

Therefore the supplier-direct workflow should preferably operate as:

> **Supplier → EU-compliant shipping arrangement → customer**

rather than:

> **Supplier → ambiguous cross-border parcel → customer who discovers customs/fees later**

The customer experience should be:

**price shown clearly → order → tracking → predictable delivery → no surprise customs bill.**

## Phase 2 fulfilment

As volume grows:

**Korean supplier → bulk EU import → European 3PL → customer**

Keep fastest-moving SKUs in the EU while retaining supplier-direct/pre-order fulfilment for long-tail products.

# 9. Important legal principle for supplier-direct selling

Even when a Korean/Asian supplier ships directly to the consumer, **KUMMIE remains the customer-facing retailer** and cannot assume that the supplier's responsibilities replace KUMMIE's own obligations.

The supplier agreement should define:

- authenticity;
- product compliance;
- manufacturer information;
- responsible-person/importer information where applicable;
- safety documentation;
- packaging information;
- customs arrangements;
- shipping SLA;
- lost-package responsibility;
- damaged-product responsibility;
- returns;
- refunds;
- recalls;
- defective products;
- IP warranty/indemnity;
- data handling.

# 11. Official merchandise and trademark exhaustion

KUMMIE should distinguish three sourcing situations.

### A. Genuine goods already lawfully placed on the EEA market

Generally the cleanest resale route, provided authenticity and other product requirements are documented.

### B. Genuine Korean goods bought in Korea

Do **not** assume that authenticity alone gives automatic EU resale rights.

EU trademark exhaustion is generally tied to goods being placed on the EEA market with the trademark owner's consent. Genuine goods first placed outside the EEA may raise parallel-import/trademark issues. The safest commercial routes are EU/EEA-authorised distribution or documented authorisation for EEA import/resale.

### C. Unauthorised branded reproductions

Do not make these a normal KUMMIE product line.

The business should protect itself through supplier due diligence, invoices and written warranties.

# 12. EU product-compliance framework

KUMMIE's operating model should classify every SKU before publication.

## 12.1 General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR)

The EU General Product Safety Regulation applies to consumer products and contains specific obligations for online/distance offers. Article 19 requires online product offers to display specified manufacturer information and, where relevant, responsible-person information, product identification and applicable warnings/safety information. [EUR-Lex — Regulation (EU) 2023/988](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj/eng)

## 12.2 Toys/figures

If a figure/product is a toy, EU toy-safety requirements can apply. Toys placed on the EU market require the applicable conformity assessment and CE marking, together with traceability, safety information and required warnings. [European Commission — Toys](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/toys/placing-toys-eu-market_en)

The product taxonomy should therefore distinguish:

- adult collectible/display product;
- children's toy;
- plush/soft toy;
- decorative object.

Do not rely on the supplier's category alone.

## 12.3 Electronics/lightsticks

Electronic products may trigger additional EU requirements depending on design and function, potentially including CE, EMC, electrical safety, RoHS, WEEE, batteries and GPSR requirements.

For such products, KUMMIE should require the supplier/manufacturer documentation before listing.

## 12.4 Textiles/apparel

Textiles sold to EU consumers generally need fibre-composition labelling and the relevant information must be provided in the official language(s) required by the country where the product is made available. [Your Europe — Textile label](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/product-rules-compliance/textiles-and-footwear/textile-label/index_en.htm)

## 12.5 Cosmetics

Cosmetics should not be part of phase-1 complexity unless supplied through a compliant EU route. The EU cosmetics regime requires an EU-based responsible person for products placed on the EU market and uses the CPNP notification system. [European Commission — Cosmetics](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/cosmetics/legislation_en)

## 12.6 Packaging

The EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation began applying across the EU on **12 August 2026**. KUMMIE therefore needs packaging/EPR processes from the start instead of treating packaging as an afterthought. [European Commission — PPWR](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/new-eu-rules-packaging-enter-application-2026-08-11_en)

# 13. Consumer rights

For EU consumer e-commerce, KUMMIE should design its policies and checkout around:

- clear total pricing;
- delivery information;
- withdrawal/returns rules;
- complaint handling;
- defective product handling;
- privacy information;
- payment information.

EU online purchases generally carry a **14-day withdrawal right** subject to the legal exceptions, and consumer goods carry a **minimum two-year legal guarantee** under the EU framework. [European Commission — Online shopping rights](https://commission.europa.eu/digital-life/protecting-you-when-buying-online_en) / [Your Europe — Guarantees](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/shopping/guarantees/index_en.htm)

The operating model should therefore reserve cash for returns, damaged goods and warranty cases.

# 14. VAT operating principle

The final tax/accounting implementation requires Bulgarian professional advice, but the platform should be architected for EU cross-border B2C VAT from day one.

The EU cross-border distance-sales framework uses a **€10,000 EU-wide threshold** for the relevant intra-EU B2C distance sales/TBE services under the conditions described by the European Commission, after which destination-country VAT rules apply; OSS can be used to simplify reporting. [European Commission — VAT OSS](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/)

Bulgaria's mandatory VAT registration threshold was reduced to **BGN 100,000** from 1 April 2025 under the Bulgarian VAT Act. [Bulgarian National Revenue Agency](https://nra.bg/wps/portal/nra/actualno/registracia-dds-april)

The financial model in this document deliberately uses **revenue excluding VAT** and excludes accounting/tax charges as requested.

# 26. Unit economics dashboard

The most important metrics are:

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

`CAC = sales + performance marketing spend / new customers acquired`

### Average Order Value (AOV)

`AOV = total net sales / number of orders`

### Gross margin

Target blended improvement:

**42% → 45% → 48%**

### Repeat purchase rate

Target direction:

**increase every quarter**.

K-pop gives the business an inherent repeat-purchase opportunity because of:

- comebacks;
- album variants;
- official merchandise releases;
- concerts;
- collector behaviour;
- gifting.

### Contribution margin

Track revenue after:

- product/landed cost;
- payment fee;
- returns/chargebacks;
- variable fulfilment leakage;
- marketing directly attributable to orders.

This is more useful for decision-making than gross margin alone.

# 33. What should NOT be done

## Do not start with a permanent physical store

It converts variable costs into large fixed costs.

## Do not build a huge catalogue

Start with demand, not inventory prestige.

## Do not depend on unlicensed branded merchandise

It may produce attractive theoretical margins but creates avoidable IP risk.

## Do not build the mobile app before proving the web business

The website should prove retention first.

## Do not treat all Asian suppliers as equivalent

Supplier quality, authorization, documentation, product safety and customs capability matter more than headline wholesale price.

## Do not make the child the commercial asset

Use her perspective, not her private identity.

# Appendix A — Recommended product classification workflow

Before any SKU becomes visible to customers:

1. **What is it?** — apparel / paper / accessory / electronic / toy / cosmetic / other.
2. **Who made it?** — manufacturer and country.
3. **Who owns the IP?** — KUMMIE / licensed third party / public domain / unknown.
4. **Is it official?** — if yes, document source and authorization.
5. **Where was it first placed on the market?** — important for genuine branded imports.
6. **Which EU requirements apply?**
7. **Which warnings/labels are required?**
8. **Who is responsible in the EU?**
9. **What is the landed cost?**
10. **What is the target margin?**
11. **What is the return/defect risk?**
12. **Only then: publish the SKU.**
